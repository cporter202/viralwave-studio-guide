# Scheduling & Publishing: Queue the Month in One Sitting

You've batched a month of posts. Now get them out of your head and onto a schedule. This is a 30-minute job, once a month.

## The scheduling session (step by step)

1. **Open your calendar CSV** — the output of `scripts/generate-calendar.py` or the [template](../templates/30-day-content-calendar.csv), now filled with finished posts.
2. **Attach visuals** — every row needs an image or video file ready. If a visual is missing, the post doesn't ship. No exceptions; missing visuals are how calendars die.
3. **Load your scheduler** — Meta Business Suite (free, covers Facebook + Instagram), TikTok's web scheduler (free), or a multi-platform tool for everything in one place.
4. **Schedule in pillar order, not date order** — do all Teach posts, then all Show posts, etc. Your brain stays in one mode and you move faster.
5. **Set it and close the tab.** The month is now handled.

## Cadence math: how much to post

Posting daily on every platform burns most people out. Be strategic instead:

**The minimum effective dose:**
- Pick **2 primary platforms** where your buyers actually are. Post there 4–5x/week.
- Pick **1–2 secondary platforms**. Repurpose the same content there 2–3x/week.
- Everything else: cross-post when it's free, ignore when it's not.

**A realistic 30-post month:**
- 20 posts → primary platforms (the same post can go to both)
- 10 posts → secondary platforms (repurposed versions)
- Total creation: 30 posts. Total placements: 40–50. That's the repurposing multiplier from [prompt 14](03-prompt-library.md) at work.

## Platform specifics

### Instagram + Facebook — Meta Business Suite (free)
- Schedule Reels, carousels, single images, and stories from desktop.
- Best posting windows are generally 7–9am and 12–1pm in your audience's timezone — but your own analytics beat any generic advice after 30 days.
- Reels get discovery; carousels get saves; stories get loyalty. Run all three.

### TikTok
- Native scheduler at tiktok.com (upload → schedule, up to 10 days out) or any multi-platform tool for longer horizons.
- Post natively when you can — the algorithm mildly prefers it.
- Raw beats polished. Your "Show" pillar phone clips are your best TikTok content.

### LinkedIn
- Native scheduling on desktop (clock icon in the post composer).
- Tuesday–Thursday mornings perform best for B2B. Text posts and document carousels (PDF uploads) are the current reach winners.
- Company pages get a fraction of personal profile reach — founders should post personally.

### X / Threads
- X has native scheduling; Threads needs a third-party tool.
- These are conversation platforms. Schedule the posts, but show up for the replies — that's where the value is.

### Pinterest
- Native scheduler, and evergreen content lives for months. Worth the extra 10 minutes to add keyword-rich titles and descriptions.
- One blog post → 5 pins is the standard multiplier.

### YouTube
- Schedule Shorts and long-form in YouTube Studio.
- Shorts are the discovery engine; use them to feed the long-form videos.

## The daily 5 minutes (the part you don't automate)

Scheduling handles publishing. It doesn't handle **being a human**. Every day:

- Reply to every comment on your posts (the algorithm notices; so do people)
- Spend 5 minutes commenting genuinely on 3–5 accounts in your niche
- Answer DMs

That's it. Five to ten minutes. This is the highest-ROI content activity there is, and it's the one thing automation should never touch.

## Common scheduling mistakes

- **Scheduling at midnight.** Dead hours get dead reach. Use your audience's morning or lunch.
- **Posting and ghosting.** A scheduled post you never engage with underperforms one you show up for.
- **Same caption everywhere.** Tweak the hook per platform — LinkedIn and TikTok are different rooms.
- **No visual, no post.** Text-only tanks on visual platforms. Attach the image.
- **Forgetting stories.** Stories take 30 seconds to schedule and keep you top-of-feed between main posts.
